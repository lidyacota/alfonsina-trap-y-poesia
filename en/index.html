About the project

"Alfonsina: Trap y Poesía" (Alfonsina: Trap and Poetry) is a transmedia homage to the Argentine poet Alfonsina Storni (1892–1938). Eight poems from her 1918 collection "El dulce daño" are recontextualized within trap, reggaeton, and dembow through AI-assisted music production. The poems remain intact, word for word, in their original Spanish: what shifts is the medium, the context, and therefore their meaning.

Conceived as a digital album-book, the piece operates as an intersemiotic translation, carrying each poem from the printed page into sound, voice, and image. Every track unfolds as a page that brings together the music, presented in video form; the technical prompt that shaped its sound; and the poem reactivated as animated visual poetry. Created in Mexico City in 2026 by Lidya Cota, a Mexican transmedia artist, the work is situated within the digital literature produced by Latin American women.

At its core is a manifesto on artificial intelligence in artistic creation. It reclaims AI not as automatism but as an instrument directed by a curatorial sensibility; it holds that the value of the work lies not in the tool but in its direction; it frames the prompt as deliberate art direction; and it declares that technology, in the hands of a woman creator, is not a neutral instrument. The website does not host the work: it is the work itself.

Manifesto

(for a Transmedia Remediation)

I. The Collision of Worlds: Avant-Garde and Reactivation

"Alfonsina: Trap y Poesía" is not merely a musical setting; it is a hybrid piece that escapes traditional linearity in order to inhabit the scriptural through sound, voice, and image. The project operates as a cultural and intersemiotic translation, in which the voice of the Argentine poet Alfonsina Storni—born on May 22, 1892, in Sala Capriasca, Switzerland—is recontextualized within the ecosystem of trap and reggaeton.

The proposal is a transmedia remediation in which the digital medium is, in effect, the message (see Marshall McLuhan). By injecting Storni's lyric poetry into rhythms historically undervalued by hegemonic culture, this work seeks to dissolve the boundary between academic intellectualism and urban popular drive—a boundary that, to a great extent, was always a class boundary. The collision between the sobriety of the lyric text and the aggressiveness of dembow is not a gesture of easy contrast: it is the demonstration that Alfonsina's sensibility was, in essence, a perpetual avant-garde that the canon contained without being able to neutralize.

II. The Selection: "El dulce daño" (1918)

The curation centers on "El dulce daño", published in 1918 by Editorial Cooperativa Buenos Aires. Written when Alfonsina was twenty-six and facing single motherhood in a society that punished her for it, the collection explores an eroticism and a passionate charge that remain, more than a century later, uncomfortably relevant.

The operation is, in a certain sense, Borgesian: the poem remains identical to the letter, but by inhabiting a new time and a new sonic materiality, its meaning shifts. "Capricho" ("Whim"), historically read as a romantic lament, reveals its true meaning under the rhythm of neoperreo and industrial reggaeton: a political and gender subversion that its original context stifled. The musical genre does not illustrate the poem; it allows the poem to reveal the irony that was always there.

III. The Synthetic Brush: Technology as a Precision Instrument

This work reclaims the use of artificial intelligence not as automatism, but as an instrument of sonic experimentation directed by a specific curatorial sensibility. The debate over whether photography "competed" with painting was settled long ago; today it is evident that technical media do not invalidate artistic creation, but compel it to become more precise. The value of this work lies not in the use of the tool, but in its direction: in the decisions that precede and condition each result.

The programming of technical prompts—specifying BPMs, Chacalón flow textures, or linguistic glitch distortions—functions as deliberate art direction:

"Primavera" ("Spring") demanded the explosiveness of Dominican dembow to translate the physical vitality of the heart in the poem.

"¡Oh, tú!" ("Oh, You!") uses cartoon trap to treat words as sound objects, drawing out a linguistic humor latent in the original text.

"Risas" ("Laughter") finds in Mexican reggaeton and heavy 808 bass the rawness needed to raise pain to the point of a guffaw, without resolving it into either one.

IV. Sensory Cartographies and Visual Poetry

The project presents itself as a digital album-book. The website does not host the work: it is the work itself. Navigation imitates the turning of pages, but is enriched by an interactivity that turns reading into a synesthetic experience: the text responds to rhythm; the letter moves with meaning.

In "Dime" ("Tell Me"), visual poetry carries this principle to its most precise formulation: the letters buzz to the rhythm of the "camoatí zumbador", the buzzing camoatí wasp, matching semantic meaning with programmed animation. This is not a decorative device. It is a wager on restoring to the written word its character as a physical event—something the page, in its immobility, had domesticated.

V. We Women: Creation, Motherhood, and Overcoming the Gap

This project is also a vital stance. We women who are creators, mothers, and technology specialists continue to face a gender gap that seeks to confine our capabilities within the borders of art or of science, rarely both at once. To claim mastery of the technological avant-garde in order to intervene in the work of a woman who defied her time is not a merely symbolic gesture: it is a continuity, a way of demonstrating that this history is not archived but inhabited.

The wager on democratizing "high poetry" (whose most exquisite example in this curation would perhaps be the alexandrine sonnet "Tu dulzura" ["Your Sweetness"]) does not stem from the belief that reggaeton needs to be legitimized by Storni, but from the opposite conviction: that Storni gains something real when her words circulate through the genres that belong to new generations by cultural right. Technology, in the hands of a woman creator, is not a neutral instrument; it is the brush of a sensibility that demands to occupy the digital public sphere with honesty and with risk.

VI. Conclusion

"Alfonsina: Trap y Poesía" is a tribute to the word that refuses to remain static. It embraces the risk of hybridization not as provocation but as method: the conviction that the melancholy of the twentieth century and the technological power of the twenty-first can vibrate, when articulated with precision, on a single frequency.
